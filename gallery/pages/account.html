Account screens

The shared screens of an app built on @cplieger/access, from @cplieger/ui/account, each running here on a fake client. A screen fills the element the app gives it, takes the app's words for any answer, and shows a method only while the app mounts it and the server has it on.

Sign-in

mountSignIn(panel, options) fills the gate page's panel: the password form, then a passkey and single sign-on under a rule, then the recovery hint in the panel's foot. The line under the panel is the app's own.

The sign-in page

RRelease tracker

Help for this screen

Every method on, after a session ended. Type the password "wrong" for a refused sign-in, "busy" for a busy server, "limit" for a rate limit, or a username with a space. Each press of Sign in with a passkey answers the next outcome: ran out of time, not registered here, closed prompt, then signed in.

One owner's words

The app's recovery hint as a node, its place for linking, and a single sign-on identity that no account uses, in the words the app passed for it.

A wrong password

A danger notice that never says which half was wrong, with focus on the password and its text selected.

Too many attempts

A countdown from the answer's Retry-After. Sign in and the passkey are held with aria-disabled until it ends; only its start and its end are said.

Fields left empty

Two empty fields: the summary lists each as a link, and nothing is sent.

A passkey that ran out of time

A warning notice; a closed prompt shows nothing.

Single sign-on did not finish

The provider sent the browser back with an error. The screen shows its code's notice and removes it from the address.

Only a passkey

Password sign-in off: the only method is the primary, with no rule above it.

Reading the state

The panel is busy with the form's shape until the server says which methods are on.

The app is not answering

The state read failed. Retry reads it again.

Sign-in turned off

No method is on.

QR code

qrCode(text) from @cplieger/ui/qr draws a text as a QR code in the page, so the secret in an authenticator link never leaves it. The key beside it, in groups of four, is the way in for a person who cannot scan.

An authenticator link

Or type this key
JBSW Y3DP EHPK 3PXP JBSW Y3DP EHPK 3PXP
A version 6 code at level M, dark on light in both themes and under forced colours, hidden from assistive technology since the key beside it says the same.
